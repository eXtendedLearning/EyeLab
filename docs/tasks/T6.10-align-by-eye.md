# T6.10 — The view offset set by eye, and a solid drawing

> Written 2026-11-22, built as 0.4.013. Run 1 (2026-11-23): a rigid view offset cannot align the drawing at arm's length (the
> error is depth-dependent); solid style preferred. Next: T6.11 (`docs/tasks/T6.11-spaam-taps.md`). Parent: T6.9 run 1 (`docs/tasks/T6.9-overlay-b3b.md`).
> One run of about 5 minutes (the alignment step can end early with Done). Needs the 25 mm ChArUco board (5×7, 19 mm
> markers, ids 0-16) and plate L (markers 40-43, 20 mm), tilted about 45°.

## Why

T6.9 run 1:
- the drawing was room-fixed to a few millimetres;
- Jack called it "very stable", more than on the Surface;
- but it was *clearly off* the markers, by a constant offset.

The camera side is verified:
- the Beam Pro's vendor Eye intrinsics equal the Surface's;
- `rgb_from_head` is 28 mm ahead of the head and rotated 1.3°;
- the device chain equals Python's to 0.000 mm.

What remains is the display: XREAL's eye and display model against Jack's eyes. On the Surface this was a view offset
of about 1.3°, set by eye (`docs/BEAMPRO-HANDOVER-2026-11-10.md`, PC gate `ViewOffset.cs`).

Jack also said: "our artefacts are clearly 2D even if in a 3D environment". The flat outlines carry stereo and motion
parallax, but no thickness, shading or depth extent. A solid style tests whether those help.

## The chain

```
world = head(cam_ts) · view_offset · rgb_from_head · F·cam·F
```

`view_offset` is a rigid correction in the head frame (Unity: x right, y up, z forward):
- translation in mm, rotation as yaw, pitch and roll in degrees;
- applied between the head and the camera, so it holds when the wearer walks;
- saved to `files/calibration/view_offset.json` in the app's storage and loaded at the start of the next align run.

`overlay.csv` logs the offset each pose was drawn with:
- `off_tx_mm`, `off_ty_mm`, `off_tz_mm`, `off_qx` … `off_qw`;
- `style` (flat or solid).

The report rebuilds the chain with it.

### Two kinds of correction

| control | what it does | grows with distance |
|---|---|---|
| **TURN** (default) | rotates the drawing about the head origin: R' = dq·R, t' = dq·t. Left/right = yaw, up/down = pitch, roll left/right | yes: the shift is the angle times the distance |
| **MOVE** | shifts the drawing in the head frame: left/right = x, up/down = y, nearer/farther = z | no |

A display boresight error is an angle, so TURN should fix it at every distance. If the drawing is right at 50 cm after
TURN but off at 35 cm or 1 m, part of the error is a shift (an eye position or IPD error), and MOVE is needed.

Steps:
- coarse: 0.5° or 5 mm;
- fine: 0.1° or 1 mm.

**Zero** clears the offset. **Save** writes it. **Done** ends the alignment step early.

## The solid style

| | flat (T6.9) | solid (T6.10) |
|---|---|---|
| outline | 2 mm strips, flat in the marker plane | 2.5 mm square bars |
| depth cue | stereo and motion parallax only | plus a 25 mm post with a 5 mm cap out of each marker along its outward normal, the way an impact direction will be drawn |
| shading | one colour | faces shaded by a fixed light (vertex colours, unlit), depth-tested (`Resources/EyeLabVertexColor.shader`) |

The posts point towards the viewer at the first sighting, since a face that is seen faces the camera. Green while live,
as in T6.9.

### What the one-eye check separates

| left only | right only | reading |
|---|---|---|
| off the same way | off the same way | a boresight error: TURN |
| off in opposite directions, both eyes in between | | a stereo or IPD error: the wearer's eyes against the display model; TURN cannot fix both eyes |
| one eye on, the other off | | the dominant eye decides what "on" looks like; a stereo error |

Please also say in the chat which way the squares sat (left, right, up, down, nearer) with each eye.

## Done when

- **Aligned:** after the alignment, the board at 50 cm is rated *on the markers* or *a few mm off*, still and moving.
- **Distance:** at 35 cm and 1 m it is rated the same. Otherwise the error is not a pure angle, and the report's
  angle-to-shift line gives the size of the rest.
- **One eye:** the answers place the error as a boresight error or a stereo error.
- **Saved:** `view_offset.json` holds the offset, and the next align run starts from it (`view_offset_start`).
- **Style:** Jack's answer on flat against solid decides the drawing used for the first `.unv` geometry.
- **Next step named:**
  - if aligned at every distance: a lock filter (One Euro, as on the Surface), and the first EyeLab geometry from a
    `.unv` file drawn in the chosen style;
  - if not: per-eye display model or IPD work.

### Reading

A rigid head-frame offset is the wrong model at arm's length. The error grows as the target comes nearer, and a
correction set at one distance fails at another. That is the signature of a **viewpoint error**: the eye's real centre
of projection is not where the SDK's per-eye render camera sits. The angular error of such an error scales with
`(1/d − 1/D)`, with `D` the display's focal distance (several metres for the One series). So it vanishes far away and
dominates in the 35-70 cm range where hammer testing happens.

Three things add to it:
- **Focal rivalry:** the display's focus is fixed at a few metres, while the board at 50 cm needs about 2 dioptres more.
  The eye cannot hold both sharp, which matches the "HUD" impression (Condino et al., IEEE TBME 2020: HoloLens,
  focus 2 m, errors up to 5.9 mm at arm's length).
- **The board's periodic grid:** 17 like squares let binocular fusion lock onto the wrong pair (the wallpaper effect).
  Plate L's four distinct markers on two faces cannot do that.
- **What Jack's saved correction mostly did:** the 43 mm "nearer" scales the drawing by about 7 % at 660 mm. That is a
  size knob, and it hints at a per-eye field-of-view (projection scale) error as well as a position error. Neither is a
  rigid head-frame offset.
