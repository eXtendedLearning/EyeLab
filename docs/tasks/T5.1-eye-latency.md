## Why

EyeLab must hold a drawn wireframe on a physical structure to millimetres through XREAL One Pro
optical see-through glasses. The head tracking alone cannot do it.

- **6DoF position drifts:** 10 cm in 30 s standing, metres while walking, at a busy venue
  (guide §18).
- **3DoF has no position.**

So the Eye camera has to keep correcting the overlay.

- **Optical see-through AR targets about 20 ms motion-to-photon,** with detection
  thresholds down to about 3 ms: Jerald & Whitton, IEEE VR 2009; Azuma 1997; Lincoln et al.,
  IEEE TVCG 2016.
- **Our camera correction is 70 ms to 0.5 s old when it is sent** (a sawtooth, §23). The pose
  service adds 65-100 ms on top.

**What the 0.3.005 session showed.** For a *fixed* structure, an old correction costs little if
its age is known exactly: head-tracking drift over 0.3 s is about 1 mm standing. Three things
cost accuracy, in this order:

1. **A wrong age.**
   - 0.3.005 assumes a constant 160 ms.
   - The true age varies by about ±150 ms per frame.
   - Pairing each frame with its own age, tested offline, halves the turning error.
2. **Blind time.**
   - Every stream restart leaves about 2 s without a lock.
   - In still phases 28-42 % of the time sits in lock gaps over 1 s.
3. **The decoder's throughput.**
   - It delivers 16-21 of 29.8 fps even on a cool Surface.
   - That is why the queue grows and the restarts are needed.

Raw latency still matters for anything that moves, and for how fast drift is corrected.

**Goal, in order:**

1. pair every frame with its true age;
2. cut the blind time;
3. cut the latency as far as the hardware allows, measuring every stage;
4. keep the registration at least as good as 0.3.005.

## What is known (measured, 14 Oct - 6 Nov)

**The stream.**

- 1280x720 at 29.8 fps, **compressed**: about 60-77 KB per frame (native log `rgb recv`).
- **Decoded on the host CPU** by the XREAL SDK. The SDK ships FFmpeg (`avcodec-60.dll`,
  `avformat-60.dll`, `avutil-58.dll`, `swscale-7.dll`) next to `XREALXRPlugin.dll`.
- **It delivers only 16-21 fps to Unity, even at full clock with a fan** (`perf.csv`
  `cam_fps`, 6 Nov).
- Frames reach Unity as YUV_420_888 with **rows bottom-up**.
- `cam_ts` is in ns on a device clock. It is strictly increasing and steps 33.5 ms.

**The decoder queue.**

- **The SDK hands every frame over in order.** When decoding is slower than 30 fps the
  queue grows without bound: 101 s of lag after 137 s on a hot Surface; 0.07-0.11 s per
  second on a cool one.
- **The application cannot skip it:** a draining reader found at most one frame waiting.
- **Stopping and restarting the camera empties it.**
  - About 0.8 s off, then about 1.05 s to the first lock: a 2.0 s gap between locks (§23).
  - **Never restart less than 0.6 s after a stop.** A 0.18 s gap killed the camera for the
    rest of a run (`NativeRGBCamera+Start` failures).
- **Stream mode (0.3.002 and later):**
  - capture continuously;
  - restart when frames fall 0.3 s behind, every 4.5-6 s;
  - send the newest frame, up to 8 Hz.
- **In still phases 28 % (3DoF) and 42 % (6DoF) of the time sits in lock gaps over 1 s.**

**Frame age at sending: a base of 70-80 ms plus a queue age of 0-0.48 s** (§23).

- **The constant estimate (0.3.004 and earlier).**
  - `python -m xreal_gates latency RUN` fitted one constant delay by consistency:
    140-200 ms in five runs.
  - 0.3.005 compensates with that constant: `cam_latency`, default 160 ms; `-camLatency`;
    keys `,` `.`.
- **The age is not constant.**
  - The *queue age* is `t_host − cam_ts` above its rolling ±20 s minimum.
  - It ramps from 0 to 0.3-0.48 s inside every stream window: median 150-180 ms, p90
    320 ms.
  - Host and device clocks drift only about 20 ms in 5 min.
- **Per-frame pairing at `base + queue age`** minimises the turning jump at base 70-80 ms
  in all three stream runs (`latency RUN` prints both):

  | | fixed delay | per-frame age |
  |---|---|---|
  | lock-to-lock jump while turning | 4.8-5.4 mm | 2.6-3.0 mm |
  | lock-to-lock jump while still | 1.2-1.6 mm | 0.9-1.0 mm |

- **Unity already has the signal.** `CameraProbe.OnFrame` computes `t − cam_ts·1e-9` per
  frame (`LagGrowth` is that minus the first value).
- **The components of the 70-80 ms base are unknown:** exposure, on-glasses encode, USB,
  FFmpeg decode, Unity texture upload, our Y-plane copy.

**Correction round trip after sending: 65-100 ms.**

- **The service:** Python pose service over a local WebSocket,
  `python/xreal_gates/pose_service.py`, ADR-001 Step C.
- **Detection and PnP:** 51-54 ms median, p90 79-87 (`pose_service.csv`, `ms`).
  - Plates run the validation saddle detector (`validation/detect.py locate_points`),
    which is the slow part.
- **Round trip:** 65-73 ms median, p90 91-103.
- **One request in flight at a time,** so about 4.8 frames/s are sent, below the 8 Hz cap.

**Host.**

- **Surface Pro 7,** a fanless i5 that throttles when hot. With a fan the light runtime
  holds full clock for 5-7 min.
- **The CPU clock can sit at 83-97 % when lightly loaded and cold.** That is not throttling
  (§22).
- **The thermal-zone temperature does not track the throttle.**
  - In a 12.6 min demo the clock fell to 130 % (min 84 %) and camera frames to 7 fps, while
    the hottest zone stayed at 52 °C.
  - The 0.3.005 heat rule checks ≥ 65 °C first and never fired (§23).
- **The desktop cannot drive the glasses:** no XR on the desktop.

**Registration** (0.3.005, guide §20-23).

- **Eye calibration:** RMS 0.47 px; fx 623.9; vendor fx 2.7 % short.
- **Lock filters.** One Euro is the default.

  | 6DoF still blocks | jitter | error | rating |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | One Euro | 1.2 mm/s | 1.3 mm | 4/5 |
  | hysteresis | 1.1 mm/s | 2.7 mm | 3.5 |
  | latest | 3.3 mm/s | 0.7 mm | 2.5 |

- **The view offset is a boresight angle** of about 1.3°: yaw +0.5°, pitch +1.2°.
  - It was set twice independently: once as a shift (x +8, y −16 mm at 0.73 m), once as an
    angle. The two agree.
  - Keep it in angle form.

**Baseline to beat** (0.3.005, plate A, 6 Nov compare runs; `filters`, `latency`, §23):

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| still-block jitter / error, One Euro, 6DoF | 1.2 mm/s / 1.3 mm |
| lock-to-lock jump while turning, as run (fixed 160 ms) | 5.0-6.2 mm |
| lock-to-lock jump while still | 1.2-1.6 mm |
| still-phase time in lock gaps > 1 s | 42 % (6DoF), 28 % (3DoF) |
| detection / round trip, median | 54 / 73 ms |

## Constraints (from AGENTS.md; do not relax)

- **No firmware updates, no USB-configuration changes.** No unofficial NCM/UVC camera
  routes; ADR-003 governs those.
- **Do not modify or redistribute vendor packages or binaries.** That covers `com.xreal.xr*`
  and the FFmpeg DLLs. Calling public APIs or exported functions is allowed.
- **Entering XR on the desktop is forbidden.** All XR runs happen on the Surface, with Jack.
- **You may build the gate players with the Unity CLI** (scoped exception in AGENTS.md).
  Play mode needs Jack's go-ahead.
- **Process each camera frame once** (`frame_packet.FramePacket`).
- **`IArucoPoseBridge` stays the only way the app gets poses.**
- **Versioning.**
  - Run `python tools/bump_version.py` once per hand-off and use the commit prefix `X.Y.ZZZ`.
  - This task ends with `python tools/bump_version.py minor` → **0.4.000**, a `[0.4.000]`
    CHANGELOG heading and tag `v0.4.000`.
- **Each hand-off:** commit text for Jack, plus guide, CHANGELOG and CONTEXT updates.
- **Builds are gitignored.** Tell Jack the zip path under `unity_projects/xreal_pc_gates/Builds/`
  to copy to the Surface; only `LATEST_BUILD.txt` travels with the commit.

## Work plan

### Phase 0 — Per-frame frame age (first; small, the evidence is in hand)

1. **In Unity, give each frame its own age** at the moment it is taken for sending:
   - `D = t_frame_arrived − cam_ts·1e-9`, already computed in `CameraProbe.OnFrame`;
   - `D_min`, a causal rolling minimum of `D` over the last 20 s, carried across stream
     windows;
   - `age = base + (D − D_min) + (now − t_frame_arrived)`, with `base` defaulting to 75 ms.
2. **Pair the frame with `HeadAt(now − age)`** instead of `now − cam_latency`.
   - Raise `HeadHistorySeconds` from 1.0 to at least 1.5 s so the oldest frames still find
     a pose.
3. **Keep `-camLatency` and keys `,` `.`,** which now adjust `base`. Log `cam_age` per lock:
   base, queue age, total.
4. **Teach `latency RUN` to read the logged per-frame age.** On a new run it should then find
   a base residual near 0, and per-frame ≈ fixed.
5. **Done when** a Surface compare run in 3DoF and 6DoF shows, against the baseline table:
   - the median turning jump at or below 3 mm;
   - the still error no worse.

> **Status (2026-11-07, build 0.3.007):** implemented and built; waiting for the Surface
> compare runs (guide §24). Two deviations, both from the data:
>
> - **The base defaults to 90 ms, not 75.** Replaying the Unity estimator on the 2-6 Nov runs
>   from `camera.csv` puts its best base at 85-100 ms. The 70-80 ms was measured from the
>   service's receipt time, which already includes the copy and send.
> - **The head history is 2.0 s** (≥ 1.5 s asked), so Phase 2 can raise the flush threshold
>   to 1.0 s without touching it.
>
> The per-lock age is logged as fields of the `lock` event (`age_ms`, `base_ms`, `queue_ms`,
> `hold_ms`, `t_send`), not as a separate `cam_age` event.

### Phase 1 — Measure every stage (no behaviour change)

1. **Host-side timestamps per frame,** from `Time.realtimeSinceStartupAsDouble` / a
   Stopwatch. Log them as one `frame_timing` row per sent frame:
   - frame available in the SDK (`TryGetRGBCameraFrame` true);
   - texture updated (`FrameArrived`);
   - our copy done;
   - WebSocket send;
   - service receive, detection start and end, reply received;
   - overlay applied.
2. **An exact device-clock to host-clock mapping for `cam_ts`.** Phase 0's rolling minimum
   gives the age relative to the freshest frame; the absolute base still comes from a fit.
   - Investigate the exported natives `GetHMDTimeNanos` / `GetHeadPoseAtTime` in
     `XREALXRPlugin.dll` (they are exported; see §15).
   - Recover signatures carefully: read-only calls, behind try/catch, logged.
   - If `cam_ts` and HMD time share a clock, then `frame_age = hmd_now − cam_ts` exactly, and
     `GetHeadPoseAtTime(cam_ts)` gives the capture-time pose directly. That replaces `base`.
3. **An optical end-to-end measurement.**
   - Show a millisecond clock on the Surface screen with the Eye looking at it. Log what the
     app saw at each receipt: camera-to-app latency, independent of the head.
   - Optionally, film glasses and screen with a phone in 240 fps slow motion for
     motion-to-photon.
4. **Done when** a table in the guide lists every stage's median and p90 latency, with its
   method, over at least one 3-minute stream-mode run on the Surface.

### Phase 2 — Cut the parts we own

- **Blind time first.**
  - With the per-frame age in place, an older frame is still correct for a fixed target. The
    0.3 s flush threshold is no longer forced by accuracy.
  - Measure the trade-off on the Surface: blind fraction against age, for `StreamFlushLag`
    0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0 s and for `StreamWindowSeconds`. Pick the setting that minimises
    still-phase error, not age.
  - Try to shorten the 1.05 s to the first lock after a restart: send the first frame at
    once, and keep the service warm.
- **Detection.**
  - Plates: do the cheap markers + PnP first and reply at once. Refine with saddles at a
    lower rate, or only when still.
  - Board: ChArUco only.
  - Crop or down-sample around the predicted target: a region of interest from the last pose.
  - Consider moving detection in-process: ADR-001 Step A/B, behind `IArucoPoseBridge`, with
    the same tests. Target: correction round trip ≤ 25 ms.
- **Pipelining.** Allow a second request in flight (two worker threads in the service) so
  the send rate reaches the 8 Hz cap. Keep "each frame once" (`FramePacket`).
- **Copy and transport.**
  - Avoid the 0.9 MB managed copy and send per frame: shared memory or a native plugin.
  - Or detect on the half-resolution Y plane where accuracy allows. Check it against the
    baseline.
- **Prediction.** Use the head history and the per-frame age to predict the target's pose at
  display time.
- **Heat signal.** Replace the zone-temperature rule with what matters: received camera fps and
  queue growth (e.g. hot when cam fps < 10 for 20 s).
- **Done when:**
  - still-phase time in lock gaps over 1 s is at most half the baseline;
  - the median correction latency (exposure to overlay applied) is at least halved against
    Phase 1;
  - registration error and jitter are no worse than the baseline.

  Check with `python -m xreal_gates filters`, `latency`, `chain`.

### Phase 3 — Investigate the decoder (vendor-side; no binary changes)

The decoder caps everything above: at 16-21 of 29.8 fps it forces the restarts. A hardware
decode path, or a lower stream resolution or rate, would remove the restarts altogether.

- **Decoder settings.** Find out whether the SDK exposes them through public or exported API:
  hardware decode (D3D11VA/QSV), threads, low-delay flags, a lower resolution or rate. Read the
  C# wrappers in `Library/PackageCache/com.xreal.xr*` and the export tables.
- **Earlier frames.** Find out whether frames can be had before the Unity texture path.
  `DrainingCameraSource` already uses `XREALPlugin.TryAcquireLatestImage` /
  `TryGetRGBCameraDataPlane`.
- **Record what is impossible too,** with evidence. If the decoder is fixed, say so and stop
  there.
- **Done when** the findings, possible or not with the reason, are in the guide; anything
  usable is implemented and measured as in Phase 2.

### Phase 4 — Validate and cut 0.4.000

1. **Surface session (Jack):** compare 3DoF and 6DoF with the new pipeline, plus one 10-minute
   demo (heat).
2. **Gates:**
   - correction latency reported per stage;
   - no camera death;
   - no false or missed heat fallback;
   - still-phase error and jitter no worse than the baseline;
   - turning jump at or below 3 mm;
   - blind time at most half the baseline.
3. **Write-up and cut:** write §24 of the guide, update CAPABILITIES rows 19-20, then the MINOR
   cut 0.4.000 (CHANGELOG heading, tag).
