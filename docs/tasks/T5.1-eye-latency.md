# T5.1 — Eye camera latency: measure it, cut it, cut a MINOR (0.4.000)

> Self-contained plan for a coding agent, written 2026-11-03 at build 0.3.005. It hands over
> the latency work on the XREAL PC-beta gate project `unity_projects/xreal_pc_gates/`. Read
> `AGENTS.md` first (rules, hardware, commits), then `docs/XREAL-PC-GATES-G1-G3.md` §15-22 (how
> the camera path behaves) and `docs/HARDWARE.md`. Everything stated as measured below comes
> from logs under `unity_projects/xreal_pc_gates/GateRuns/runs/` (gitignored; on the desktop).

## Why

EyeLab must hold a drawn wireframe on a physical structure to millimetres through XREAL One Pro
optical see-through glasses. The head tracking alone cannot do it.

- **6DoF position drifts.** 10 cm in 30 s standing, metres while walking, at a busy venue;
  guide §18.
- **3DoF has no position.**

So the Eye camera has to keep correcting the overlay. How useful that is depends on how
old each camera frame is and how often it arrives.

- **Optical see-through AR targets about 20 ms motion-to-photon,** with detection
  thresholds down to about 3 ms: Jerald & Whitton, IEEE VR 2009; Azuma 1997; Lincoln et
  al., IEEE TVCG 2016.
- **Our camera correction is about 160 ms old when it is sent,** and the pose service adds
  60-90 ms on top.

Goal: cut the correction latency as far as the hardware allows, measure every stage, and keep
the registration at least as good as 0.3.005.

## What is known (measured, 14 Oct - 2 Nov)

**The stream.**

- 1280x720 at 29.8 fps, **compressed**: about 60-77 KB per frame (native log `rgb recv`).
- Decoded **on the host CPU** by the XREAL SDK. The SDK ships FFmpeg (`avcodec-60.dll`,
  `avformat-60.dll`, `avutil-58.dll`, `swscale-7.dll`) next to `XREALXRPlugin.dll`.
- Frames reach Unity as YUV_420_888 with **rows bottom-up**.
- `cam_ts` is in ns on a device clock; it is strictly increasing and steps 33.5 ms.

**The decoder queue.**

- The SDK hands every frame over in order. When decoding is slower than 30 fps the queue
  grows without bound: 101 s of lag after 137 s on a hot Surface.
- The application cannot skip it: a draining reader found at most one frame waiting.
- **Stopping and restarting the camera empties it.** The first frame then takes 1.1 s.
- 0.3.002 and later use **stream mode**: capture continuously, restart when frames fall
  0.3 s behind (about every 4 s), send the newest frame up to 8 Hz.
- **Never restart less than 0.6 s after a stop.** A 0.18 s gap killed the camera for the
  rest of a run (`NativeRGBCamera+Start` failures).

**Frame age at sending: about 160 ms.**

- Estimated from consistency: `python -m xreal_gates latency RUN`. Pairing each frame with
  the head pose L earlier minimises lock-to-lock jumps while turning at L = 140-200 ms, in
  five runs.
- 0.3.005 compensates with `cam_latency` (default 160 ms; `-camLatency`; keys `,` `.`).
- The **components** are unknown: exposure, on-glasses encode, USB, FFmpeg decode, SDK
  queue, Unity texture upload, our Y-plane copy.

**Correction round trip after sending: 60-90 ms.**

- Python pose service over a local WebSocket: `python/xreal_gates/pose_service.py`,
  ADR-001 Step C.
- Detection and PnP take 45-125 ms per frame (`pose_service.csv`, `ms`). Plates run the
  validation saddle detector (`validation/detect.py locate_points`), which is the slow part.

**Host.**

- Surface Pro 7, fanless i5, throttles when hot. With a fan the light runtime holds full
  clock for 5 min; full-video decoding throttles within seconds.
- The CPU clock can sit at 83-97 % when lightly loaded and cold, which is not
  throttling (§22).
- The desktop cannot drive the glasses: no XR on the desktop.

**Registration** (0.3.005, guide §20-22).

- Eye calibration: RMS 0.47 px; fx 623.9; vendor fx 2.7 % short.
- Lock filters: One Euro and hysteresis, about 3 mm/s jitter still, 2-3 mm error.
- View offset in the head frame, set by eye (about 1.2° down).

## Constraints (from AGENTS.md; do not relax)

- **No firmware updates, no USB-configuration changes.** No unofficial NCM/UVC camera
  routes; ADR-003 governs those.
- **Do not modify or redistribute vendor packages or binaries.** That covers
  `com.xreal.xr*` and the FFmpeg DLLs. Calling public APIs or exported functions is allowed.
- **Entering XR on the desktop is forbidden.** All XR runs happen on the Surface, with Jack.
- **You may build the gate players with the Unity CLI** (scoped exception in AGENTS.md);
  play mode needs Jack's go-ahead.
- **Process each camera frame once** (`frame_packet.FramePacket`). `IArucoPoseBridge` stays
  the only way the app gets poses.
- **Versioning.** Run `python tools/bump_version.py` once per hand-off and use the commit
  prefix `X.Y.ZZZ`. This task ends with `python tools/bump_version.py minor` → **0.4.000**,
  a `[0.4.000]` CHANGELOG heading and tag `v0.4.000`.
- **Each hand-off:** commit text for Jack, plus guide, CHANGELOG and CONTEXT updates.

## Work plan

### Phase 1 — Measure every stage (no behaviour change)

1. **Host-side timestamps** per frame, from `Time.realtimeSinceStartupAsDouble` / a
   Stopwatch:
   - frame available in the SDK (`TryGetRGBCameraFrame` true);
   - texture updated (`FrameArrived`);
   - our copy done;
   - WebSocket send;
   - service receive, detection start and end, reply received;
   - overlay applied.

   Log them as one `frame_timing` row per sent frame.
2. **A device-clock to host-clock mapping for `cam_ts`.**
   - Investigate the exported natives `GetHMDTimeNanos` / `GetHeadPoseAtTime` in
     `XREALXRPlugin.dll` (they are exported; see §15). Recover signatures carefully: read-only
     calls, behind try/catch, logged.
   - If `cam_ts` and HMD time share a clock, then `frame_age = hmd_now - cam_ts` exactly.
     `GetHeadPoseAtTime(cam_ts)` would then give the capture-time pose directly, replacing
     the fixed `cam_latency`.
   - If they do not, fit the offset from the consistency estimate.
3. **An optical end-to-end measurement.**
   - Show a millisecond clock on the Surface screen with the Eye looking at it, and log what
     the app saw at each receipt: camera-to-app latency, independent of the head.
   - Optionally, film glasses and screen with a phone in 240 fps slow motion for
     motion-to-photon.
4. **Done when:** a table in the guide lists every stage's median and p90 latency, with its
   method, over at least one 3-minute stream-mode run on the Surface.

### Phase 2 — Cut the parts we own

- **Detection.** Plates: do the cheap markers + PnP first and reply at once; refine with
  saddles at a lower rate or only when still. Board: ChArUco only.
  - Crop or down-sample around the predicted target (a region of interest from the last pose).
  - Consider moving detection in-process: ADR-001 Step A/B, behind `IArucoPoseBridge`, with
    the same tests. Target: correction round trip ≤ 25 ms.
- **Copy and transport.** Avoid the 0.9 MB managed copy and send per frame: shared memory
  or a native plugin. Or detect on the half-resolution Y plane where accuracy allows; check
  it against the 0.3.005 error numbers.
- **Queue.** Tune `StreamFlushLag` and `StreamWindowSeconds` against the Phase 1 numbers.
- **Prediction.** Use the 1 s head history and the measured frame age to predict the
  target's pose at display time.
- **Done when:** the median correction latency (exposure to overlay applied) is at least
  halved against Phase 1, with registration error and jitter no worse than 0.3.005. Check
  with `python -m xreal_gates filters`, `latency`, `chain`.

### Phase 3 — Investigate the decoder (vendor-side; no binary changes)

- Find out whether the SDK exposes decoder settings through public or exported API:
  hardware decode (D3D11VA/QSV), threads, low-delay flags, a lower resolution or rate. Read
  the C# wrappers in `Library/PackageCache/com.xreal.xr*` and the export tables.
- Find out whether frames can be had before the Unity texture path. `DrainingCameraSource`
  already uses `XREALPlugin.TryAcquireLatestImage` / `TryGetRGBCameraDataPlane`.
- Record what is impossible, too, with evidence. If the decoder is fixed, say so and stop
  there.
- **Done when:** the findings, possible or not with the reason, are in the guide; anything
  usable is implemented and measured as in Phase 2.

### Phase 4 — Validate and cut 0.4.000

1. Surface session (Jack): compare 3DoF and 6DoF with the new pipeline, plus one G5 plate A
   run.
2. Gates:
   - correction latency is reported per stage;
   - no camera death;
   - no false heat fallback;
   - still-phase error and jitter no worse than 0.3.005;
   - turning error lower than 0.3.005.
3. Write §23 of the guide, update CAPABILITIES rows 19-20, then the MINOR cut 0.4.000 (CHANGELOG
   heading, tag).
