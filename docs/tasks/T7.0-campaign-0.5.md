## 2. What the literature says to measure

No standard defines the evaluation of an OST AR guidance system for a manual task. The measurement set below combines the
sources that come closest.

| quantity | definition used here | how measured here | sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy = trueness + precision** | trueness: mean error vector (bias); precision: spread (SD per axis, covariance ellipse). Report bias, SD, RMSE, median and 95th percentile of the radial error (R95) | C1 (camera side), C4 (display), C6 (task) | ISO 5725-1:2023 |
| **Benchmark indicators for vision-based registration and tracking** | reliability (accuracy, robustness), temporality (latency, rate), trial set (physical object instances) | C1-C3; trial set = the plates and sheets, described with their measured geometry | ISO/IEC 18520:2019 |
| **Ground-truth uncertainty** | U (k = 2) of every reference point, TUR = tolerance / U ≥ 4 | caliper on the scale bars, as in `validation.md` | JCGM 100:2008; ANSI/NCSL Z540.3-2006 |
| **Perceived registration (OST)** | only the user sees the final result, so OST registration is evaluated with the user in the loop (alignment or pointing tasks) or a camera at the eye | C4 taps and checks, C6 task; eye camera postponed | Grubert et al. 2018; Gilson et al. 2008 |
| **Overlay stability** | jitter (high-frequency shake), drift (slow displacement), jumps (sudden relocation), swim (sway with head motion) | C3 still / turn / walk; head-frame and room-frame overlay position from the logs | Vassallo et al. 2017; Scargill et al. 2021 |
| **Latency** | age of each correction at use, per stage; motion-to-photon not measured (display side), reported as a limitation | stage timing and frame age logged in every run; timecode run | Jerald & Whitton 2009; Di Luca 2010 |
| **Near-field perceptual limits** | OST displays focus at ≥ 2 m, so virtual and real content cannot both be in focus at arm's length; parallax between camera and eye | recorded as factors: viewing distance at each hit, dominant eye, comfort ratings | Condino et al. 2020; Ferrari et al. 2024 |
| **Task performance** | error of each hit against the nominal point, time per hit | C6 | Condino et al. 2020 (connect-the-dots under AR); HL2 alignment studies (2.7 ± 1.3 mm) |
| **Workload, usability, sickness** | NASA-TLX, SUS, SSQ | C6, per participant | Hart & Staveland 1988; Brooke 1996; Kennedy et al. 1993 |
| **Environment** | illuminance, background, temperature affect HMD measurements and must be reported | lux and temperature logged per block | Guo et al. 2022 |

**Where EyeLab sits.** The AR-in-EMA work found (Hossain, Hanson & Moreu; WaveAR 2022; Carter, Sakr & Sadhu 2024)
*visualises* mode shapes or responses. None of the sources found measures how accurately AR *guides* sensor or impact
placement. That is the gap the campaign addresses; the claim must be re-checked with a proper literature search before
the paper.

## 3. What EMA needs: a tolerance derived from the mode shape

No standard found sets a tolerance on accelerometer or impact location (ISO 7626-5 covers impact excitation, not
placement). The earlier 1 mm aim is replaced by a tolerance derived from the highest mode of interest. This derivation is
ours, to discuss with the senior colleagues and to state as such in the paper.

For a mode shape that varies locally like a sine of wavelength λ (two node spacings), a placement error Δx changes the
measured amplitude by at most `Δφ / φ_max ≈ (2π / λ) Δx`, where the slope is largest (at the nodes). For a relative
error ε:

`Δx_tol = ε · λ / (2π)`

| λ (= 2 × node spacing) | ε = 2 % | ε = 5 % |
|---|---|---|
| 100 mm | 0.32 mm | 0.80 mm |
| 200 mm | 0.64 mm | 1.6 mm |
| 500 mm | 1.6 mm | 4.0 mm |
| 1000 mm | 3.2 mm | 8.0 mm |

Two physical floors sit next to it: the hammer tip contact patch and the accelerometer base are both several mm across.
The paper reports the measured R95 against this table: "usable for modes with λ above X mm at ε = 5 %".

## 4. One APK, data-driven, processed live

### 4.1 One APK

- **The campaign APK is frozen before the lab** (0.5.0xx, tag `campaign-2026-12`) and its hash is logged in every run.
  It is installed and dry-run at home (§10). No build in the lab.
- **Everything that might change is data, not code.** Pushed once by adb into the app's files folder, read at start:
  - `campaign/protocol.json`: gates, steps, durations, cue texts, factors, randomisation seeds;
  - `campaign/sheets/<id>.json`: each sheet's targets (model frame, metres) and layout id;
  - overlay style (crosshair size, gap, line width, colour), display model choice (SDK / pooled model), filter and
    detection settings.
  Every run logs the protocol's version id and hash. A change during the campaign is a new protocol version, written
  in the campaign log with the reason.
- **Fallback without a laptop:** two protocol variants (normal, reduced) are preloaded and selectable on the Beam Pro
  screen.
- **Experimenter page on the Beam Pro screen:** participant id (P00 = Jack, P01…), condition, sheet id (also read from
  the sheet's ID marker), start, stop, "bad trial", and three quick notes (lock lost, tool slipped, other).
- **Hit events:** a Bluetooth presenter held by the experimenter ("next target") plus the tap's sound onset on the
  Beam Pro microphone (hit time). The dot on paper does not depend on either; the events only link each hit to the
  log (lock state, markers in view, head distance) and time it.

### 4.2 Processed while we take

- The laptop (Surface, adb, offline venv) runs `tools/watch_beampro_run.ps1`, which pulls each finished run.
- `python -m campaign quick RUN` writes a one-page summary in under 2 min and appends a row to
  `campaign_runs/campaign_log.csv`:
  - C1/C2: plate found %, poses/s, err_mm median / p95 per session;
  - C3: still jitter, turning jump, frame age, late frames, temperature;
  - C4: fresh-tap error per eye, ratings;
  - C6: per sheet, the Eye estimate at once; after the phone photo (dropped into `campaign_runs/photos/`), the error
    of every hit, bias and R95, against the participant's printed-point control.
- **Participants do not see their own errors** until they have finished (no learning from feedback).
- **Decision points:** after C1's first 6 sessions, after Jack's C6 pilot (end of day 1), after participant 1. Changes
  allowed: step durations, target count per sheet, overlay style, distances. Not allowed after participant 1: anything
  that changes C6's conditions.
- **Backups:** after each block, the runs, photos and the campaign log to the SSD and to OneDrive.

## 5. Printed material (one marker frame for everything)

All sheets share one **marker frame F40**: four ArUco markers of 40 mm (DICT_4X4_50, ids 30-33) in the corners of an
A4 landscape page, centres 28 mm from the edges (8 mm of paper outside them), plus an X scale bar (150 mm, bottom) and
a Y scale bar (90 mm, left) placed between the markers. The same frame tracks in C1, C4 and C6, so the camera term measured in C1 applies to C6. A 40 mm marker is
about 50 px at 0.5 m for the Eye (fx 627), against 25 px for the 20 mm markers of plate A, which was too small at
75 cm (T6.7). Each page is taped flat on the lab's glass (slightly larger than A4) against two corner stops.

| layout | content | used in |
|---|---|---|
| **A40** | frame + 5×4 saddle grid at 40 mm (plate A's grid) | C1, C2, C3 |
| **M** (marking) | frame + 8 border saddles (scan reference) + sheet-ID marker (id 40+) + nothing else; its 12 targets exist only in its JSON | C6 AR |
| **P** (printed) | frame + border saddles + sheet ID + the 12 targets printed as numbered crosshairs | C6 control |
| **T** (practice) | as M, 6 targets, not analysed | C6 practice |

- Targets per sheet: 12, random (seeded per sheet), at least 30 mm apart and 15 mm from markers, saddles and edges,
  in the central area (about 200 × 120 mm). Each sheet has its own set, so nobody can learn positions.
- The scale bars are measured with the caliper before the campaign (TUR against the tolerance, `validation.md`).
- The layout code gets a collision check (markers, bars, saddles, targets), which plate A lacked.

**Quantities to print:** A40 × 3; per participant M × 4 + P × 2 + T × 1, plus for Jack and one more M × 4 for the
SDK condition; 30 % spares. With Jack and 5 colleagues that is about 65 sheets plus the 3 A40.

## 6. Gates

Each gate lists purpose, set-up, procedure, time, what is logged, and when it is done. Times are lab times.

### C0 — Readiness (each morning, 30 min day 1, 20 min day 2)

- Versions: APK hash, protocol id, Beam Pro and glasses firmware (unchanged since T6.0), Eye calibration
  (`beampro_eye`, T6.13) and display model id (T6.12 pooled).
- Beam Pro battery ≥ 80 % (charger on between participants), temperature, glasses fit.
- Quick tape check: Eye pose of A40 at 50.0 and 100.0 cm, scale within 3 % (T6.13 rule).
- Display check (T6.12 Check only) by Jack at 35/60/100 cm: "on the markers" or "a few mm off".
- Room: illuminance at the table (lux), temperature, photo of the set-up with a scale.
- **Done when** every item is recorded and passes; otherwise stop and fix before C1.

### C1 — Camera-side accuracy, static (glasses on a tripod; 1.5 h)

- **Purpose:** RQ1. Error of the Eye pose expressed as overlay error on the plate, independent of the user and of the
  display.
- **Set-up:** A40 on the glass, flat on the table; glasses clamped on a tripod head (not worn); diffuse light.
- **Factors:** distance 350 / 500 / 750 / 1000 mm × angle 0 / 30 / 50° × 3 repetitions (rebuilt each time) = 36
  sessions of 15 s. Distance and angle shown live from the pose; one session per condition also checked with the tape and
  an inclinometer.
- **Logged:** every Eye frame's pose and detection; every 3rd frame saved (Y plane) for the offline analysis.
- **Analysis:** the validation package on the saved Eye frames (saddles as ground truth, independent of EyeLab's pose):
  err_mm per node, bias map, pose jitter, detection rate (`validation/`, with the Beam Pro Eye calibration).
- **Done when** all 36 sessions are analysed and the quick check shows ≥ 95 % plate found up to 750 mm.

### C2 — Robustness envelope (45 min)

- **Purpose:** RQ1 variety: where detection and accuracy degrade.
- **Runs (tripod, A40, 500 mm, 30°, 30 s each):** light at three levels (room full / half / one lamp, lux logged);
  one and two markers covered by a hand; angle 60° and 70°; distance to the detection limit (1.25, 1.5 m).
- **Worn run (2 min):** head turns at slow / normal / fast pace while looking at the plate (named cues).
- **Logged and reported:** plate found %, markers per pose, poses/s, err_mm where a pose exists.

### C3 — Stability and timing (worn, 30 min, plus one endurance run)

- **Purpose:** RQ2.
- **Runs (Jack, A40 at about 50 cm):**
  - still 60 s standing, hands off;
  - turn and nod with named cues 60 s;
  - step back and forth 1 m, 60 s;
  - detection off 30 s (overlay held by head tracking alone: drift);
  - timecode 60 s (Eye → app).
- **Logged (every run already does):** frame age per frame, stage times, poses/s, late Unity frames, head pose, the
  overlay's room position.
- **Endurance:** one 30-min run in the background of C6 (participant 1): temperature, fps, battery.
- **Reported:** still jitter (mm/s and high-pass mm), drift without detection (mm in 30 s), lock-to-lock jump while
  turning, frame age median / p90, late frames %.

### C4 — Display model and perceived registration (per participant, 10 min)

- **Purpose:** RQ3. Does the pooled display model (T6.12: a property of the device) hold for other people?
- **Procedure:**
  1. eye dominance test (Miles), recorded;
  2. **Check only** with the pooled model, no taps: 6 check taps per eye on the board at 35, 60, 100 cm (crosshair
     in one eye, as T6.11), plus a rating on A40;
  3. only if the check error is above 5 mm in either eye: a fresh calibration round (taps) for that participant, and
     C6 uses it.
- **Logged:** check-tap residuals per eye and distance (mm at the target), with the model in use.
- **Reported:** per participant the error of the pooled model, and whether a personal calibration was needed.

### C5 — SDK against calibrated display (Jack and at most one more, 15 min each)

- C6's AR procedure with 2 M sheets under the SDK display and 2 under the pooled model, order SDK-cal-cal-SDK.
- **Reported:** bias and R95 per display model on the same task: what the display calibration buys in the task.

### C6 — Hammering simulation (per participant, about 65 min)

- **Purpose:** RQ4 and RQ5.
- **Task:** the sheet lies on the glass on the table. The participant stands, holds the mock hammer, and taps each target
  with it; the pen refill in the tip leaves a dot at the tip centre.
  - **AR (condition A):** M sheet. The glasses show one crosshair at a time (20 mm arms, 4 mm central gap, number), in
    random order; nothing is printed on the sheet.
  - **Printed (condition P):** P sheet, numbered crosshairs printed; the glasses are worn but draw only "hit point 7".
    Wearing them in both conditions keeps the optics (tint, field of view) the same, so A − P isolates registration.
  - The experimenter presses "next" when the participant is ready; the tap's sound times the hit.
- **After each sheet:** a **review look** (the participant looks straight down at the sheet from about 30 cm for 3 s;
  Eye frames saved for the first estimate), then the phone photo from a fixed stand, then the sheet goes into its
  envelope for the scan.
- **Order per participant** (counterbalanced across participants):

  | step | min |
  |---|---|
  | information, consent, SSQ before | 10 |
  | C4 (dominance, check, calibration if needed) | 10 |
  | practice sheet T (AR, 6 targets) | 3 |
  | 6 sheets: A P A A P A (or P A A P A A), 12 targets each | 25 |
  | NASA-TLX for A and for P, SUS for EyeLab, SSQ after, 5 comfort/usefulness ratings, 3 open questions | 15 |
  | buffer | 2 |

- **Logged per hit:** sheet and target id, time, lock state and markers in view, head distance and angle to the sheet,
  display model, overlay style, protocol id; per sheet: the review-look frames and the photo.
- **Measured hit position:** dot centroid in the sheet frame from (1) the Eye review frames (first estimate, about
  0.5 mm/px at 30 cm, with a 1 mm tip), (2) the phone photo (homography on the border saddles and marker corners;
  phone lens calibrated once with the ChArUco board, squares 34.17 mm), (3) the flatbed scan, final.
  The three are compared on every dot: the Eye estimate is a result too ("can EyeLab check its own work?").
- **Reported:** per condition and participant: bias vector with 95 % CI, SD per axis, radial median and R95, time per
  hit; the system share `σ_sys = √(σ_A² − σ_P²)` per axis; error against trial number (learning); error against lock
  state and viewing distance.

### C7 — 3D stretch (Jack only, if time is left)

- Plate L on its 90° bracket, 6 targets per face, AR only. Shows whether the grazing face changes the picture.

### C8 — Close of each day (30 min)

- Backups (SSD + OneDrive), campaign log reviewed, notes, sheets counted and photographed, glasses and Beam Pro charged.

## 8. Analysis: error budget and EMA readiness

The hit error in the AR condition is modelled as independent terms added in quadrature, each measured by its own gate:

| term | measured in | at the C6 geometry |
|---|---|---|
| camera pose → overlay on the plate | C1 | 500 mm, 30-50° |
| display model (eye ↔ display) | C4 check taps | per participant |
| dynamics (jitter, age while the head moves) | C3 | still and small motion |
| person + tool | C6 printed condition | per participant |

- **Consistency check:** the sum predicts the AR error per participant; agreement (within the CIs) validates the
  budget, and the largest term is the answer to "why".
- **EMA readiness:** R95 of the AR condition against the tolerance table of §3, and the same for the budget without
  the person term (what a perfect operator would get).
- **Statistics:** few participants, so per-participant summaries first, then paired comparisons A vs P (Wilcoxon
  signed-rank) and a mixed model (radial error ~ condition + trial index, participant random) as support, not as the
  headline.

### 13.5 What was dropped for this campaign, and why

- **High-speed camera and photodiode.** The Eye sees the world, not the display, so its timecode measures camera →
  app only; display latency (app → light out of the glasses) needs an observer outside: a high-speed camera or a
  photodiode (Di Luca 2010). Not needed for this campaign: C6 with its printed control measures what reaches the user's
  eye end to end, and latency is reported per stage on the Beam Pro plus the timecode, with display latency stated as
  a limitation. Later, Jack's Arduino + LED + photodiode at home suffice.
- **The Galaxy M55's slow motion** is not confirmed in a trusted spec (check `com.android.capture.fps` in a file).
- **The Beam Pro's rear cameras** (two 50 MP, 1080p at 60 fps 3D video per the press; no slow-motion mode found) cannot
  film the glasses: the Beam Pro is the host that drives them.
- **Eye camera behind the lenses** (Gilson et al. 2008; Grubert et al. 2018): the objective measure of the display
  term. Postponed; C6's printed control gives the end-to-end figure, and T6.12's taps the display term with the user in
  the loop. The holder (mannequin head or 3D-printed) and a small UVC camera with a fixed-focus M12 lens are the
  later items.
- **A3 plates and a 3D structure:** no A3 glass; the 3D structure (plate L on a machined 90° bracket) is C7, stretch.
