# T6.11 — Per-eye calibration taps (SPAAM)

> Written 2026-11-23, built as 0.4.014. Run 1 (2026-11-23): the eye-position model predicts a fresh tap to 2-3 mm
> against 12-14 mm for the SDK; next T6.12 (`docs/tasks/T6.12-eye-calibration.md`). Parent: T6.10 run 1 (`docs/tasks/T6.10-align-by-eye.md`).
> One run of about 4 minutes. Needs the 25 mm ChArUco board (5×7, ids 0-16), upright on a table. The real point is the
> centre of the board's **middle square**: marker 8, at (62.5, 87.5) mm in the board frame.

## Why

T6.10 run 1 showed that a rigid head-frame offset cannot align the drawing at arm's length:
- the error grows as the target comes nearer (38 cm *clearly off*, 96 cm *a few mm off*);
- each eye's drawing is off the opposite way. Jack, 2026-11-23: "right eye grid was left of marker, left eye grid was
  right of marker". The two lines of sight cross in front of the board, so the drawing floats nearer ("like a HUD").

That is the signature of the eye's centre of projection differing from the SDK's per-eye render camera. In the
off-axis pinhole model of an optical see-through display (Cutolo et al. 2020), an eye `C_x` off sideways moves the
drawing by about `C_x (1 − d/D)` at distance `d`, with `D` the display's focal distance. That is zero at `D` and
largest at arm's length.

Drawing right of the point in the left eye and left of it in the right eye puts both eyes **inward** of the SDK's: Jack's
eye spacing is smaller than the SDK's. The glasses are a One Pro M (57-66 mm, centre 63 mm). Jack does not know his own
IPD.

SPAAM (Tuceryan & Navab 2000; survey: Grubert et al. 2017) measures the eye-display model one eye at a time. A
crosshair is fixed in one eye's display; the wearer moves the head until it covers a tracked real point, and taps. Taps
at several display positions and depths constrain the eye's position, the display's distance and its angular scale.

### The model

In the SDK eye's frame (x right, y up, z forward):
- a display pixel (u, v) (tan units) is the point `X = D (s·u + p_x, s·v + p_y, 1)`;
- the wearer sees a real point `P` aligned with it when the true eye centre `C`, `X` and `P` are collinear.

Parameters per eye:
- `C` (3);
- `1/D` (1);
- `s` (1);
- `p` (2).

With `C = 0`, `s = 1`, `p = 0` this is the SDK's own model. The real point comes from the trusted board poses of the two
seconds before each tap, carried into the room by the overlay's chain, then into the head at the tap.

## Protocol (about 4 min, voice-guided, self-paced)

| step | s | what Jack does |
|---|---|---|
| s_ready | 14 | board upright on the table, about 60 cm |
| chk_L | 12 | the crosshair should be in the **left** eye only: close each eye in turn, answer on the screen |
| d60_place | 10 | stand with the board at about 60 cm |
| d60_L_1 … d60_L_4 | ≤ 40 each | right eye closed: move the head until the crosshair's centre is on the centre of the middle square, then tap. The crosshair moves to the next display position |
| d60_R_1 … d60_R_4 | ≤ 40 each | the same with the left eye closed |
| d35_…, d100_… | | the same at about 35 cm and about 1 m |

That gives 24 taps, 12 per eye. Notes for Jack:
- Look at the square, not the crosshair: the crosshair will be a little blurred (the display focuses far away).
- Tap only when it sits still on the centre. If a tap was off, press **Last tap was bad**.

## Done when

- **Eye check:** the crosshair is in the left eye only during chk_L (else the eye mask or the eye index is wrong).
- **Taps:** at least 10 good taps per eye with the board seen.
- **SDK error by depth:** the signed error per eye at 35, 60 and 100 cm shows the depth trend. The expected sign from
  Jack's report: the left eye's drawing right of the point, the right eye's left of it, larger at 35 cm.
- **Fit:** the model's residual per eye is clearly below the SDK model's, with an eye centre and spacing in a
  plausible range (eye spacing 50-75 mm, eye 0-30 mm from the SDK's).
- **Next step named:**
  - if the fit holds: T6.12 draws each eye with the fitted model (a per-eye correction in the overlay shader) and repeats
    the T6.10 distance checks;
  - if not: more taps, or a full 3x4 per eye.

**Found while analysing:** the board rule (12+ markers trusted) applied on the device only to the drawing variants. The
60 cm and two 1 m taps had 16-marker board poses marked untrusted, so the first report used 14 taps. The report now
applies the rule offline (`spaam.trusted_board`), giving 24 of 24. The device applies it from 0.4.015.

### Where the SDK draws the board's centre against where Jack sees it

| eye | 35 cm | 50 cm | 73 cm | SDK median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| left | (+8.2, −6.6) mm | (+8.1, −5.7) mm | (+9.6, −8.3) mm | 11.9 mm |
| right | (−10.7, −6.6) mm | (−10.7, −8.7) mm | (−11.4, −11.0) mm | 14.3 mm |

x is right and y up; + means the drawing is right of or above the point. Tap spread per depth: 1.6-3.6 mm.

- The error is nearly **constant in millimetres** across depths, so it grows in angle as the target comes nearer.
  That is a translation of the eye-display centre (`C (1 − d/D)` ≈ `C` at arm's length), not a boresight angle,
  which would double from 35 to 73 cm.
- Opposite signs per eye: the drawing floats in front. Both eyes' drawings are 6-11 mm low.

### The fit (display at 4 m)

| model | left eye centre from the SDK's | right | fresh-tap error (leave-one-out, median / max) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDK | 0 | 0 | 11.9 / 14.3 mm (all taps) |
| eye position only (3 per eye) | (+9.4, −7.4, −14.8) mm | (−12.1, −8.7, −12.4) mm | L 1.9 / 7.2 mm, R 2.9 / 5.4 mm |
| eye, scale, centre (6 per eye) | (+6.9, −5.2, −27.9) mm | (−9.9, −2.7, −16.6) mm | L 1.7 / 8.1 mm, R 2.5 / 3.7 mm |

- A DLT (11 parameters, textbook SPAAM) gives the same centres to 1-3 mm in x and y.
- With the display distance free, the fit runs off (scale 0.53, centre 15°): 34-73 cm cannot pin `D`, so it stays
  fixed at 4 m.

**The model predicts a tap it has not seen to 2-3 mm, against 12-14 mm for the SDK's model.** That is about the tap
noise (1.6-3.6 mm per depth), so this many taps resolve the eye model as far as the taps allow.

The fitted centres are not anatomical: 41.8 mm between them is below any adult IPD. They are the effective centres of
eye plus display, and absorb the displays' own alignment and how the glasses sit (eyes 7-9 mm lower and 12-15 mm
further back than the SDK assumes). That is enough for drawing, not a measurement of Jack's IPD.
