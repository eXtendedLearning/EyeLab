# T6.6 — Gate B3a: ArUco detection and pose on the Beam Pro's CPU

> Written 2026-11-19, built as 0.4.006. Run 1 (2026-11-19, 0.4.006): bench done, live found nothing because the
> Eye rows reached the detector bottom-up (results below). Fixed in 0.4.007; run 2 pending. Parent: ADR-0004 (accepted 2026-11-19: the pose runs on the
> device, option A2, OpenCV Android SDK in a native plugin, with a parity test). One run of about 90 s plus a
> bench of a few seconds. Needs plate A (markers 30, 31, 32; 20 mm).

## Why

ADR-0004 puts **operable** first (a stable image, the lowest latency) and **portable** second (no Beam-Pro-only
dependency on the pose path; no frame budget without a measured margin). B3a answers three questions before the
pose path is built on the device:

1. **Cost.** How long do CLAHE, detection and the board pose take on the Beam Pro's CPU, with one thread and
   with OpenCV's default threads?
2. **Margin.** Does the glasses' render loop keep 60 fps (T6.5's lean default) while detection runs on every
   Eye frame beside it? How old is a pose when it is ready (`end − cam_ts`)?
3. **Parity.** Does the device give the same markers, corners and pose as the Python pipeline on the same
   frames? Then the Python tools and tests stay valid for the device.

## The frame set (pushed by the agent)

`python -m xreal_gates b3a-set --snapshots <run>/snapshots` writes `python/beampro_runs/b3a_set/`:

- `set.json`: dictionary (DICT_4X4_50), the "balanced" detector tuning, the Eye intrinsics, plate A's marker
  corners in metres (from `config/markers/plate_A.markers.json` through `registration.marker_object_corners`), and
  the frame list;
- 16 synthetic 1280x720 frames of plate A at 0.35-1.0 m with tilts up to 35°, at known poses (`truth.json`), with
  per-pixel noise sigma 0.7 grey levels (0.4.007; it was 3.0, see run 1);
- 8 real Eye frames with plate A from run 1, flipped upright (`eyeplate_*`; `--eye-plate <run>/frames --bottom-up`):
  the cost reference, and parity on real frames with markers;
- 4 empty frames and 4 real Eye snapshots (no plate): the cost when nothing is found.

The agent pushes it with `adb push` to `files/b3a/` in the app's own storage.

The intrinsics are the 2026-10-21 PC-gate Eye calibration (1280x720, RMS 0.47 px). Parity and timing do not
depend on them, because both sides use the same K. Whether they hold for the Beam Pro's stream is B3b's question.

## The run (button **5 Detection**)

1. **Bench** (about 15-25 s; the glasses keep drawing): each set frame processed 3 times (after one warm-up) with 1, 2,
   4 and 8 OpenCV threads, on a worker thread. OpenCV's Android default is 2 threads whatever the core count (run 1).
   Output: `bench.csv`, `bench_corners.csv`.
2. **Live** (about 74 s, spoken cues):

   | step | seconds | OpenCV threads |
   |---|---|---|
   | `ready`: plate A straight ahead, about 50 cm | 8 | 2 |
   | `t2_still`: look at the plate, hold still | 15 | 2 |
   | `t2_move`: move slowly, plate in view | 12 | 2 |
   | `t2_away`: look away | 6 | 2 |
   | `t4_still` | 15 | 4 |
   | `t4_move` | 12 | 4 |
   | `t4_away` | 6 | 4 |

   Run 1 (0.4.006) used 1 thread, then OpenCV's default (2).

   - **Main thread:** acquires the newest Eye frame and copies its Y plane once (about 0.9 MB), rows reversed: the Eye
     stores them bottom-up, and the intrinsics are for the upright image (`python/xreal_gates/eyecam.py`). The time
     of this work is logged per Unity frame (`frames.csv` `eye_ms`, 0.4.007).
   - **Worker thread:** processes the newest frame. A frame that arrives while the worker is busy replaces the waiting one and counts as skipped.
   - **Output:**
     - `live.csv`: per processed frame, the times on CLOCK_MONOTONIC (= the HMD clock), the markers and the pose;
     - `live_corners.csv`;
     - `frames.csv`: every Unity frame's time, by step;
     - every 10th processed frame saved at full resolution for the parity test on real frames.

## Report

`python -m xreal_gates bp <run>` prints:

- **Bench:** per thread mode, the median and p95 of the CLAHE, detect, pose and total times, plus the time on frames without the plate.
- **Parity (bench):** whether the ids are equal, the largest corner difference in px, and the pose difference in mm and degrees against Python. Each side's error against the truth.
- **Live, per step:**
  - frames processed per second and frames skipped;
  - the share of frames with markers, and with a pose;
  - total time and wait;
  - the pose's age when ready;
  - Unity fps and the share of frames over one refresh;
  - for still steps, the pose jitter (std of t in mm).
- **Parity (Eye frames):** the same comparison on the saved real frames.

## Run 1 (2026-11-19 22:30, 0.4.006)

Jack ran it at about 67 cm from plate A (the distance Python finds once the frames are flipped). Report:
`python -m xreal_gates bp python/beampro_runs/BeamProRuns/20261119_223023_detect` (run data gitignored).

**Live detection found nothing, because the frames were mirrored.** 2175 Eye frames, 1156 processed. Plate A was
in view in most of them, but markers were found on only 2 % (false positives, ids 17 and 37), and no pose was found.

- **Cause:** `DetectProbe` copied the Y plane as delivered. The Eye stores its rows bottom-up, which the PC gates
  knew (`eyecam.py`, `beampro.decode_id`). T6.1 had recorded the Beam Pro rows as upright from snapshots, which was
  wrong (corrected there). A vertically mirrored marker does not decode.
  - A top-to-bottom flip, not a left-right one, is right: after it, plate A's axes come out upright in the camera frame
    (plate x to the right, plate y up, markers 30 and 31 at the top as configured). On a monitor in view, the window
    controls move to the top right. The radiator ends up under the window.
- **Proof:** on the saved frames flipped upright, Python finds 30, 31 and 32 and a pose at about 665 mm. Run as
  stored, the same frames give nothing. Of the 113 saved frames without markers, 64 show plate A once flipped.
- **Fix (0.4.007):** the copy reverses the rows. The report now warns when saved frames show the plate only after
  flipping.
- **Run 1 still answers several of the questions:**
  - **Live cost on real frames:** the plugin ran on every frame and the plate rarely changes the cost (desktop: 28-52 ms
    for real frames, with or without the plate found).
  - **Pose age and frame margin.**

**Bench.**

| threads (resolved) | synthetic plate A, total median/p95 | frames without plate A |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (1) | 341/351 ms | 83/146 ms |
| default (2) | 186/193 ms | 47/108 ms |

- **The synthetic frames were not representative.** Their per-pixel noise (sigma 3 grey levels) becomes about 65,000 contours
  after CLAHE and the adaptive threshold, against about 4,000 in a real Eye frame. They take 160-180 ms even on the
  desktop.
  - Fix in 0.4.007: noise 0.7, which gives about 1,500 contours and 10-17 ms on the desktop. Real upright plate frames are
    now added to the set as the cost reference.
  - The finding stands on its own: **the detector's cost rises steeply with fine image noise.** A dim, noisy scene could
    cost several times a well-lit one. Watch for this in B3b.
- **OpenCV's default on Android is 2 threads.** The plugin reports 8 CPUs, but `setNumThreads(-1)` resolves to 2, a
  cap OpenCV sets on Android against overheating. 0.4.007 tries 1, 2, 4 and 8 explicitly.

**Parity on the bench.**

- **Ids:** the same on 24/24 frames.
- **Corners:** within 0.01 px on 12 of the 16 synthetic frames with markers.
- **The other 4:** one corner each differs by 0.18-0.93 px, and the pose by up to 3.0 mm / 2.4° (plate_08, 0.7 m).
- **Neither side is closer to the truth.**
  - Python is closer on 3 of the 4 corners, the device on 1. The distances to the true projection are 0.16-1.16 px for Python and 0.35-1.22 px for the device.
  - The truth error is the same on both sides: t median 0.98 mm, r median 0.58° (device) and 0.63° (Python).
- **Reading:** the same algorithm, with floating-point differences (ARM NEON against x86 SIMD) that can move a
  sub-pixel corner refinement to a neighbouring solution. The size is within the corner noise against the truth.
  This meets "otherwise, the difference is explained". The live Eye frames agree to 0.0001 px, but only 3 frames had
  markers.

**Live.** Medians, by thread count.

| threads | processed/s | processing ms | pose age when ready | arrival + wait + processing | Unity fps | over one refresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 11.4-13.1 | 74-89 | 188-199 ms | 96 + 16 + 78 | 58.0-59.9 | 5.8-7.5 % |
| 2 | 19.6-21.8 | 44-50 | 155-164 ms | 98 + 14 + 49 | 59.8-59.9 | 5.8-9.3 % |

- **Arrival:** the Eye frame reaches the app 96 ms after exposure (arrival − `cam_ts`, the same measure as T6.1-T6.4).
  It varies from session to session: 152 ms (T6.1), 110 ms (T6.3), 133-138 ms (T6.4), now 96 ms. On the device the
  pipeline adds wait plus processing to it, about 60-95 ms.
- **Margin not met.**
  - Detection running beside the glasses raises the share of frames over one refresh (over 20.8 ms) from T6.5's 2.8 %
    (lean 0.8, no Eye stream) to 5.8-9.3 %.
  - The saved frames do not explain it: 2.7 % of the frames near a save are late, against 1.7 % elsewhere, by a 25 ms threshold.
  - 0.4.007 times the main thread's Eye work per frame, to separate the copy from CPU contention.
- **The Eye delivers 29 fps;** at 1 thread more than half of the frames are skipped.

## Done when

- **Cost known:** median and p95 of the total per frame on the Beam Pro, for 1 thread and default threads.
- **Margin:**
  - Unity stays at about 60 fps, with the share over one refresh no worse than T6.5's lean (2.8 %), while detection runs on every Eye frame;
  - otherwise, the thread count or frame rate that keeps it.
- **Age:** the pose's age when ready, against the same run's Eye frame age at arrival (arrival − `cam_ts`): what the device adds.
- **Parity:** the same ids on every frame. Corners within 0.01 px and poses within 0.01 mm of Python on the bench frames (same OpenCV version); otherwise, the difference is explained.
- **Next step named:**
  - B3b: the pose in the glasses with the head-pose correction at `cam_ts`, and the Eye intrinsics checked on the Beam Pro stream;
  - or the pipeline's cost reduced first (ROI tracking, lower resolution).
