## Why

T6.11 showed the eye-display model can be measured, but only offline. Jack (2026-11-23) asked three things:
- whether one session is enough;
- whether the calibration's quality can be seen at once, to redo it if poor;
- whether several calibrations can be averaged.

This gate answers all three on the device:

1. **Quality at once.**
   - After the taps, the Beam Pro fits the model per eye and shows its **fresh-tap error**: the leave-one-out median
     and max, in mm at the target, next to the SDK model's error on the same taps.
   - A poor round (median above about 4 mm, or one tap far off) is visible before leaving the board.
2. **Pooling.**
   - Every round's taps are appended to `calibration/spaam_taps.csv`, each round tagged with its own wearing id.
   - The fit is made twice: on **this round** and on **all rounds**.
   - More taps from several wearings average out tap noise and how the glasses happened to sit.
   - Per-wearing fits measure repeatability (`python -m xreal_gates spaam-pool`).
3. **Trying it.**
   - The board and plate L are drawn per eye with the fitted model (a vertex warp in the shader).
   - On the Beam Pro screen, the drawing switches live between **SDK**, **This round** and **All rounds**, and is
     rated at 60, 35 and 100 cm and on plate L. Each rating is logged with the model in use.

The store is seeded with T6.11 run 1's 24 taps (`spaam-export`, pushed into the app's `files/calibration/`), so the first
T6.12 run already pools two wearings.

## The model and the correction

The model is T6.11's eye-display model with the display at 4 m (`w = 1/D = 0.25`).

- **Parameters:** only the eye centre `C`, in the SDK eye's frame (x right, y up, z forward). Scale and principal point
  stay at the SDK's. In T6.11 the 6-parameter fit gained under 0.5 mm, so 3 parameters keep each round stable from 12
  taps.
- **Fit:** damped Gauss-Newton on the tap residuals in tan units; the error is reported in mm at the target.
- **Prediction:** a real point `P` in eye coordinates is seen through display position
  `uv = w·C_xy + (1 − w·C_z)·(P_xy − C_xy)/(P_z − C_z)`.
- **Drawing:** to make the eye see a drawn vertex at `P`, the SDK's own projection must put it at `uv`. So the vertex
  is drawn at `Q = (uv·P_z, P_z)` in the SDK eye's frame: same depth, shifted laterally.

On the device:
- `EyeModel.cs`: the fit, leave-one-out, the tap store, `eye_model.json`.
- `EyeWarp.cs`: per frame, the world ↔ SDK eye matrices from the head pose and `GetDevicePoseFromHead(DISPLAY_L/R)`,
  set as shader globals.
- `EyeLabVertexColor.shader`: `_EyeWarp` on the overlay material; the eye is picked by `unity_StereoEyeIndex`
  (single-pass instanced).
- **SDK mode:** the warp is the identity (`C = 0`), so the switch compares only the eye model.

The tap's real point is the board's middle square. Its head-frame position is the median of the trusted board poses of
the last 2 s, carried through the overlay chain into the head and then into the SDK eye. The board rule (12+ markers
trusted) now applies to SPAAM runs too.

## Protocol

**T6.12 Eye calibration** (about 6 min, voice-guided):

| step | s | what Jack does |
|---|---|---|
| s_ready, chk_L | 14, 12 | board at about 60 cm; the crosshair is in the **left** eye only (close each eye in turn) |
| d60/d35/d100 taps | ≤ 40 each | as T6.11: 4 taps per eye per distance, 24 in all |
| fit | 10 | read the fit on the Beam Pro screen: this round and all rounds, per eye, fresh-tap error against the SDK's |
| c60_place, c60 | 12, 25 | board at 60 cm: press **SDK**, **This round**, **All rounds** in turn, look with both eyes, rate each |
| c35, c100 | 10+25, 12+25 | the same at 35 cm and 1 m |
| l_ready, l_check | 12, 25 | plate L tilted 45°, about 60 cm: switch, move the head a little, rate each |

**T6.12 Check only** (about 3 min): s_ready, then the check steps above with the saved calibration. Use it after taking
the glasses off and on, to see whether the calibration survives a re-wearing.

## Done when

- **Fit on the device**
  - It matches Python's fit of the same taps to 0.5 mm in `C`. The report prints the device's and Python's fits side
    by side.
  - The fresh-tap error is shown on the Beam Pro.
- **Pooling**
  - The tap store holds both wearings.
  - The all-rounds fresh-tap error is at most about 3 mm per eye.
  - The two wearings' eye centres agree within about 3 mm; if not, a calibration per wearing is needed.
- **Live check**
  - At 35 and 60 cm, **This round** or **All rounds** is rated clearly better than **SDK**.
  - At 1 m all three may be close, since the error shrinks in angle there.
  - On plate L, the corrected model is not worse.
- **Check only**
  - After re-wearing, **All rounds** still rates better than **SDK**.
  - If it does not, the wearing position dominates, and a short re-tap per wearing (one depth) is the next step.
- **Next step named**
  - if the correction holds: carry the warp into the EyeLab overlay (UNV wireframe on the specimen);
  - if not: the display distance from a far tap set (2-3 m), or eye-relief cues.

## Notes

- **The fitted centres are effective, not anatomical.** The T6.11 eye spacing of 42 mm is too small for a real IPD.
  The centres include the displays' alignment and the fit of the frame on the nose.
- **Depth cues are not corrected.** Focal rivalry (display focus at about 4 m against a target at arm's length) and
  the board's periodic grid remain; the solid style helps.
- **Literature:**
  - Cutolo et al. 2020 (off-axis eye-display model);
  - Tuceryan & Navab 2000 (SPAAM);
  - Grubert et al. 2017 (survey);
  - Condino et al. 2020 (focal rivalry at arm's length; one eye no worse than two).

- **Check only** `20261123_114158_eyecheck`: after re-wearing the glasses.

### What Jack saw

| run | SDK | This round | All rounds |
|---|---|---|---|

In Jack's words: "This round" was very precise on the board but not stable. The SDK seemed more stable but clearly off.

### Three defects found

1. **The store was never written.**
   - The seed `spaam_taps.csv` had been pushed over adb, so it belonged to the shell user. The app could read it but not
     append to it (`Access ... is denied`, logged as `spaam_taps_save_error`).
   - So "All rounds" in the check-only run was T6.11's 24 taps alone, and "This round" had no model and did nothing.
2. **The seed taps were computed with the wrong extrinsic.**
   - T6.11's `spaam` variant did not log `rgb_from_head`, and the offline tap points fell back to identity. That left out a
     1.1° pitch and a 28 mm lever arm.
   - With the device's real extrinsic (the same in every run since 0.4.012):
     - the two wearings' SDK errors agree: left (+4.5, +8.8) against (+3.8, +9.4) mm, right (−15.2, +6.6) against
       (−16.2, +7.9) mm;
     - one wearing's model predicts the other's taps to 3.1-3.8 mm, against 17 mm before.
   - **The calibration carries over between wearings.** The pooling failed only because of the bad seed.
3. **The Eye camera model is 25-28 % short in focal length on the Beam Pro.** This is the main finding. *(Wrong, see
   Run 2 and T6.13 run 1: the board's squares are 34.17 mm, and the caliper value below was a marker. The symptoms below
   are real; their cause is the board's scale, not the camera's.)*
   - **Board stability:** whenever Jack walked to a new distance, the room-stability fit wanted the camera translation
     scaled by 1.34-1.39 (e.g. 49 → 5 mm std while walking from 35 cm to 1 m). That is far beyond T6.9's inconclusive
     1.14/1.50, and well determined here (translation-dominated moves of 0.3-0.5 m).
   - **The board print:** Jack measured the middle square with a caliper, 25.97 × 25.98 mm. The print is 3.9 % over the
     nominal 25 mm, which is real but does not explain 1.35.
   - **Tape check** (glasses on the table, run `20261123_124113_eyecheck`): with the lens at 500 and 1000 mm, the device's
     camera model (the Surface calibration, fx 624) puts the board's plane at 398.7 and 785.7 mm. That is 1.254× and 1.273×
     short, so fx about 790 px.
   - **A self-calibration** on 1761 saved board views from all Beam Pro runs gives fx 695. It is weak (42 % coverage, the
     board mostly square-on to the camera) but also above 624.
   - **Conclusion:** the Beam Pro's 1280×720 Eye stream does not have the field of view the vendor's intrinsics and the
     Surface calibration describe. It is about 78° wide, not 93°.

A focal-length error reprojects perfectly (AGENTS.md), so detection, parity and RMS never showed it. It accounts for
most of what T6.9-T6.12 measured:
- **Opposite-eye error.** A point placed about 25 % too near along the camera ray, seen from eyes about 32 mm either side
  of the camera, is off by about 32 × 0.27 ≈ 9 mm per eye. The two eyes are off in opposite directions, by an amount
  constant in mm with distance. That is the T6.10/T6.11 signature, and the fitted 41-42 mm "eye spacing" (with the
  board scaled back up, the spacing comes to 58-61 mm).
- **"Swims when I move."** The drawing's room position moves with the head by about a quarter of the distance change.
  It is most visible when the drawing is otherwise right.
- **T6.9's "constant offset".**

So the eye-display model fitted here partly absorbed the camera error. It is refitted after T6.13.

### Why "All rounds" was off

The round's fit, per eye:

| L | (−0.03°, +1.17°), scale 0.976 | (−0.49°, +0.81°), scale 0.978 |
